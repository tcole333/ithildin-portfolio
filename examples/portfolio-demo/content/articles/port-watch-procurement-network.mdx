## Why this example exists

This article is intentionally small. It is not here to sell a single investigation subject. It is here to show how Ithildin handles the infrastructure problem: public artifacts, hosted copies, metadata-only records, and finding-level provenance all in one reader-facing flow.

The core pattern is a deniable procurement corridor. A thin Florida vehicle appears during the key shortlist window, a UK advisory entity sits behind the services layer, and a single intermediary keeps the handoff legible only when multiple source classes are combined [Finding #9101].

## Mixed inspectability is the normal case

The easiest systems to demo are the ones where every source is public and clean. Investigative infrastructure is rarely that tidy. In this case, a public registry filing [REG:FL:L23000214567] and a procurement packet [DOCUMENTCLOUD:24402693] are fully inspectable. A hosted minute excerpt [HARBOR-LEDGER-BOARD-MINUTES-2024-11] is inspectable because Ithildin can safely publish the relevant extract. A reporting memo [PORT-WATCH-CALL-NOTES-2025-02-14] is not publicly inspectable, so the system falls back to a source record that exposes provenance, locator, excerpt, and every public use.

That is the point of the source-record layer: keep evidence accountable even when the artifact itself cannot be published [Finding #9102].

## What the demo demonstrates

Harbor Ledger's dossier shows the entity-level path from formation timing to procurement narrative [Finding #9001, #9004]. Lina Ortega's dossier shows the human intermediary path, including where the record is public and where it is only metadata-rich [Finding #9006, #9007]. Together they demonstrate why investigative tooling needs more than a link checker. It needs explicit evidence classes, occurrence tracking, and UI that does not pretend a metadata-only record is the same thing as an open public document.
